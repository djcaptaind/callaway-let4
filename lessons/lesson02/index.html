Week 2 · Lesson 2 · Making Difficult Decisions

What do you do when every choice has a cost?

Today, there may be no perfect answer. Your responsibility is to identify facts, weigh consequences, consider people, and defend a choice you can live with.

A repeatable decision process

The THINK Framework

Use these five steps before deciding. Strong judgment begins by slowing down enough to see the whole situation.

TTell the facts

What do you know? What is assumed?

HHonor your values

Which principles are in conflict?

IIdentify people affected

Who benefits? Who may be harmed?

NNotice consequences

What happens now, later, and publicly?

KKeep your integrity

Could you defend the choice to someone you respect?

Facilitator note: Ask students which step they usually skip when making decisions quickly.

Decision Lab 1

The Wallet

You find a wallet in a nearly empty parking lot. It contains an ID, credit cards, and $480 cash. Nobody appears to have seen you pick it up.

New information: The owner lives more than an hour away. A friend tells you, “They probably have insurance. Keep the cash.” Does distance, inconvenience, or peer pressure change your duty? Choose again and explain your reasoning aloud.
Facilitator note: Push beyond “because it is right.” Ask: right according to which value, and what is the foreseeable harm?
Decision Lab 2

The Closing Shift

You work after school. Thirty minutes before closing, your manager tells you to tell arriving customers that the store is sold out so the staff can leave early. The item is still in stock.

New information: You need this job to help with household expenses, and the manager controls next week’s schedule. How should personal risk affect your decision? Which option protects integrity without ignoring reality?

Citizen’s Court · Case 04-117

Safety or Privacy?

A city has repeated vandalism and assaults in public parks. Leaders propose AI-enabled cameras. Supporters expect faster investigations and deterrence. Opponents fear surveillance, unequal enforcement, and misuse of data.

EXHIBIT A

Public Safety

Residents report avoiding the park after dark.

EXHIBIT B

Privacy

The policy does not clearly limit retention or facial recognition.

EXHIBIT C

Alternatives

The same funding could support lighting, youth programs, or park staff.

Assessment lens: Evidence, empathy, ethics, consequences, and acknowledgment of alternatives.
