Week 2 · Lesson 2

Leadership is not something you are given.

It is what you choose when the moment is difficult, inconvenient, or costly.

Today you will face the same leadership decisions as LET 3, but your responsibility is greater: recommend action, guide others, protect trust, communicate tradeoffs, and anticipate second-order consequences.

Core Idea

A title gives authority. A choice reveals leadership.

A person can hold a position and avoid responsibility. Another person can have no title and still protect others, tell the truth, solve a problem, or take the first responsible step.

See the moment

Recognize that a situation needs responsible action.

Weigh the human cost

Consider who may be helped, harmed, ignored, or silenced.

Choose with courage

Act even when the responsible choice is uncomfortable.

Own the result

Accept accountability and adjust when new facts appear.

Essential Question

When does an ordinary decision become a leadership decision?

Facilitator note: Ask students to name a person who influenced them without holding an official title. Require a specific behavior—not merely a name.

Leadership Decision 1 · Loyalty vs. Accountability

The Video Everyone Is Sharing

Your closest friend sends you a video from a weekend party. In the background, a student is being humiliated while several people laugh. Your friend did not start it, but they recorded the video and posted it to a private group. By Monday morning, copies are spreading.

New information: Your friend says the targeted student has bullied others for months and insists the video “finally shows who they really are.” The targeted student has now missed two days of school.
Push the thinking: Is loyalty protecting a friend from consequences—or helping a friend face consequences responsibly?
Leadership Decision 2 · Opportunity vs. Family Responsibility

The Interview You Cannot Replace

You have a final interview for a competitive summer program that could lead to a scholarship. The interview is in 45 minutes. Your parent calls: your younger sibling has been suspended, no adult can leave work, and someone must pick them up immediately. Missing the interview likely ends your opportunity.

Your siblingYour parentThe programYour future goalsYour family’s trust
New information: This is the third time this semester that your sibling’s behavior has disrupted your plans. Your parent says, “Family comes first,” but offers no plan to prevent it from happening again.
Push the thinking: Does choosing yourself always mean selfishness? Does choosing family always mean responsible leadership?
Leadership Decision 3 · Results vs. Integrity

The Missing Fundraiser Money

Your student organization raised $3,600 for a community project. While reviewing receipts, you discover that $240 cannot be explained. The student treasurer is respected, is applying for a major scholarship, and says it was probably a paperwork mistake. The project deadline is tomorrow.

Community partnersStudent donorsThe treasurerThe organizationThe advisor
New information: The treasurer admits using the money for a family utility bill and planned to replace it after payday. Reporting it may affect the scholarship and could lead to discipline. Ignoring it could damage trust and violate school policy.
Push the thinking: Can compassion and accountability exist in the same decision? What would each look like here?
Citizen’s Court · Case 02-201

Cancel the Game—or Accept the Risk?

A high school is scheduled to host a championship game expected to generate significant revenue for student activities. On game day, the heat index is forecast to reach a dangerous level. The district policy permits play if additional water breaks and medical staff are present. Several athletes say they want to compete, while some parents demand cancellation.

EXHIBIT A

Student Safety

Heat illness can develop quickly, and athletes may minimize symptoms to remain in the game.

EXHIBIT B

Student Opportunity

For seniors, this may be their final championship and an important recruiting opportunity.

EXHIBIT C

Community Impact

Cancellation may create financial losses, travel costs, and conflict with families and supporters.

EXHIBIT D

Available Options

Move the start time, relocate indoors, postpone, shorten play, or proceed with safeguards.

Assessment lens: The strongest LET 4 answer evaluates competing priorities, recommends policy or leadership action, defends tradeoffs, anticipates second-order consequences, and explains implementation and accountability.
